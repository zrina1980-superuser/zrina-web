What should you record after a drone flight?

A simple flight log routine: conditions, batteries, equipment, issues and the next maintenance steps.

After a flight, we usually want to look at the footage and move on. But that's also the easiest time to record details that may help later: how long the flight lasted, how the battery behaved, what conditions were like and whether the equipment is ready for next time.

You don't need a long report. A few consistent notes are enough — keeping them regularly matters more than making every entry perfect.

Flight summary

  • Date and location: note the place and, if useful, approximate coordinates.
  • Flight time: record the duration and purpose, such as practice, filming or surveying a site.
  • Conditions: note wind, visibility and anything else that affected the flight.
  • Equipment: record the aircraft, controller and battery identifiers if they matter to your log.

Batteries and equipment

Record which battery you used, its starting state if you track it, and anything unusual you noticed during the flight or after landing. A short note like “check connector” is more useful than relying on memory. After the flight, inspect the equipment according to the manufacturer's instructions and note anything that needs servicing or replacement.

Problems, decisions and the next step

Was a planned flight cut short? Did a warning, connection issue or unusual behaviour appear? Record what happened and what you did. If something needs inspection, leave it as an open item rather than assuming the problem has disappeared.

Documents and privacy

Keep track of documents and deadlines relevant to your equipment and the way you fly. Rules depend on the location and flight category and can change, so always check current official sources and local requirements before making decisions. Don't publicly share locations or information that could compromise people's privacy or sensitive sites.

DroneLogbook grew from a desire to keep flights, batteries, equipment, documents and maintenance information together instead of scattered across notes and spreadsheets.

A two-minute routine: after landing, enter the basics, note anything unusual and add one specific item to check before the next flight.
